# SDG 3 Details: Good Health and Well-being

## Goal

### `1:10` Goal Definition

Ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages.

---

## Major Targets

### `1:15` Key Targets

| Target | Focus Areas |
| --- | --- |
| **3.1 Maternal Mortality** | Prenatal care, skilled birth attendants, emergency obstetrics — global MMR fell to 197/100k (2023, from 228 in 2015); still ~3x off the 2030 target of 70 (WHO/UNICEF 2025) |
| **3.2 Child Deaths** | Vaccination, nutrition, clean water — under-5 deaths fell to 4.9M in 2024 (from 6.1M in 2015); neonatal mortality 17.2/1,000, -11% since 2015 (UN IGME 2025) |
| **3.3 End Epidemics** | HIV/AIDS, TB, Malaria, Hepatitis — off-track; lenacapavir long-acting PrEP rollout (2025), R21/RTS,S malaria vaccines scaling across Africa, new TB vaccine trials (M72/AS01E) |
| **3.4 Non-communicable Diseases** | Heart disease, cancer, diabetes, mental health — 18M under-70 NCD deaths in 2021; mental health now folded into 3.4, WHO pushing primary-care integration (Lancet NCD Countdown 2030) |
| **3.5 Substance Abuse** | Alcohol, drugs, tobacco — rising focus on synthetic opioids (fentanyl crisis), vaping regulation, alcohol taxation policy |
| **3.6 Road Safety** | Traffic laws, vehicle safety, emergency response — UN Decade of Action for Road Safety 2021-2030 off-track; AEB safety-tech mandates expanding in EU/India |
| **3.7 Reproductive Health** | Family planning, sexual education, maternal care — contraceptive access gains stalling in fragile states; digital family-planning tools scaling |
| **3.8 Universal Health Coverage** | Affordable hospitals, insurance, medicines — UHC Service Coverage Index progress stalled, 4.5B people lack full coverage (WHO 2021); catastrophic health spending rising post-COVID |
| **3.9 Pollution-related Illness** | Air, water, chemical exposure reduction — ~7M deaths/yr linked to air pollution; new WHO air quality guidelines track PM2.5-attributable mortality |
| **3.d Pandemic Preparedness** | Post-COVID IHR (2005) amendments adopted 2024; WHO Pandemic Agreement adopted 2025 |
| **NEW — Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)** | WHO AMR global priorities 2025-2035 (WHA77); ~1.27M deaths/yr directly attributable to AMR (Lancet/WHO 2024) |

## Interconnections Across SDGs

### `1:295` How the 3 SDGs Connect

| SDG | Main Focus | Typical Problems | Links to Other SDGs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SDG 3 — Good Health** | Human health & well-being | Disease, mental health, healthcare access, pollution-related illness — air pollution causes ~7M deaths/yr (WHO); NCDs cause 18M under-70 deaths/yr; UHC coverage still stalled for 4.5B people | Cleaner air from decarbonization (SDG13) cuts ~7M pollution deaths/yr (WHO) · Walkable, green cities (SDG11) reduce NCD & mental-health burden and improve hospital access · Heatwaves (SDG13) raise cardiovascular/respiratory mortality especially in dense cities lacking cooling infrastructure (SDG11) |
| **SDG 11 — Sustainable Cities** | Cities & communities | Traffic, pollution, housing, disasters, waste — 1.12B people in slums globally (UN-Habitat 2024); congestion & housing financialization pricing out residents in major cities | Green, transit-oriented urban planning lowers emissions (SDG13) · Parks, clean air & sponge-city flood defenses improve health outcomes (SDG3) · C40 Cities & UN-Habitat frame compact, transit-oriented cities as the single highest-leverage intervention across all three SDGs |
| **SDG 13 — Climate Action** | Climate change | Global warming, floods, heat waves, emissions — 2025 global mean temp ~1.5°C above pre-industrial (WMO); NDCs still fall short of Paris alignment; adaptation finance gap remains large despite COP29/30 pledges | Emission cuts directly improve air quality & reduce disease burden (SDG3, IPCC AR6/WHO 2024) · Resilient infrastructure & early-warning systems (Early Warnings for All, 2027 target) reduce disaster losses in cities (SDG11) · Cities produce ~70% of energy-related CO2 (C40), making urban decarbonization core to SDG13 progress |
| **Synthesis** | Shared leverage point | Car-dependent, sprawling, fossil-fueled urban growth is the common root cause behind poor health outcomes, climate emissions, and unsafe/unequal cities | Compact, walkable, transit-oriented, green urban design simultaneously cuts emissions (13), improves air quality & reduces NCDs (3), and reduces slum sprawl & disaster exposure (11) — the design-thinking sweet spot for cross-SDG solutions (C40 Cities, UN-Habitat World Cities Report 2024/2025, WHO 2024) |
