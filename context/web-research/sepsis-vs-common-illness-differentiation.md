# Differentiating Sepsis from Common Self-Limiting Illness Using Wearable Vitals (Neonate/Infant)

**Research date:** 2026-08-11
**Purpose:** Answer the reviewer question — "elevated temperature + elevated heart rate + reduced activity could just be a cold or a normal viral fever. How does this device raise HIGH SUSPICION *specifically* for sepsis rather than false-alarming on every minor illness?" — with a research-backed answer for the [Neonatal Sepsis Danger-Sign Armband](../implementation-plans/neonatal-sepsis-armband.md).

---

## 1. Summary Answer

No single vital sign in our sensor set (temperature, HR, HRV, activity/movement, respiratory-rate proxy, perfusion index) is sepsis-specific in isolation. **Sepsis specificity comes from three things layered together, not from any one sensor or threshold:**

1. **Directionality/shape of each signal**, not just magnitude. Sepsis in neonates skews toward *hypothermia* (not fever), toward HR rise *disproportionate* to temperature change, toward HRV *pattern degradation* (not just "lower HRV" — reduced variability + transient decelerations + reduced entropy, reflecting autonomic/vagal failure under inflammatory load), toward irregular/apneic breathing (not just fast breathing), and toward *declining* peripheral perfusion (vasoconstriction/vasodilation from circulatory compromise) — each of which has a real, mechanistically distinct pattern from the corresponding change in ordinary viral illness or teething.
2. **Multi-system, multi-hour trend co-occurrence.** Common illness typically perturbs 1–2 systems and then self-resolves within a day or so, with vitals staying roughly proportional to each other. Sepsis tends to produce simultaneous, progressive deterioration across *multiple, physiologically independent* systems (thermoregulation, cardiac autonomic control, respiratory pattern, perfusion) that does not resolve and tends to worsen hour over hour. This is the core logic behind validated multi-parameter tools like PEWS and pSOFA, and it is the reason "trend over hours" beats "single threshold crossing."
3. **Honesty about ceiling.** A wearable with no blood pressure, lactate, white count, or CRP cannot make a clinical sepsis diagnosis. What it *can* legitimately claim is a statistically elevated pattern-based suspicion — the same claim the HeRO monitor and SepAl make — sufficient to trigger a triage-level "go get this checked" alert, not a diagnostic one. This is consistent with how the device is already framed in the implementation plan (§10: "screening triage aid, not a diagnostic replacement").

The strongest single differentiator in our sensor set is **HRV pattern (reduced variability + transient decelerations + reduced entropy)** combined with **perfusion index decline** — both reflect autonomic/circulatory compromise mechanisms that are part of the sepsis inflammatory cascade specifically, rather than generic "unwell" physiology. Temperature direction (hypothermia vs. fever) and HR/temperature proportionality are useful *supporting* signals but are weaker alone. The recommended device logic (§4 below) is a composite/weighted score across all of these — never a single threshold — which is also the single biggest lever we have for cutting false positives from viral URI/teething/normal fever.

## 3. Why trend/trajectory beats single-threshold reasoning (supporting evidence)

- **Pediatric Early Warning Score (PEWS)** combines behavior, cardiovascular (HR, capillary refill/color), and respiratory (RR, effort, SpO2) parameters into one score specifically because "there is unlikely to be a single early warning score... that adequately captures all types of decompensation" from any one vital alone. In sepsis-specific validation, PEWS achieved **83% sensitivity / 78% specificity** — meaningfully better than any single-vital threshold rule typically achieves — and importantly, mean PEWS score was shown to rise progressively from **5.3 at 20–24 hours before a deterioration event to 8.4 at 0–4 hours before the event**, demonstrating that the *trend slope*, not a single reading, is what carries the predictive signal. Also notable: the same PEWS score has different implications depending on underlying diagnosis (a PEWS of 5 in sepsis vs. bronchiolitis reflects different amounts of true decompensation) — reinforcing that a naive fixed-threshold sum score isn't enough; context/pattern-of-derangement matters, not just total point count.
- **pSOFA (pediatric Sequential Organ Failure Assessment)**, the pediatric adaptation of the adult sepsis-3 SOFA framework, formalizes multi-organ trend scoring (respiratory, cardiovascular, neurologic, renal, hepatic, coagulation) precisely because sepsis is defined clinically as *multi-organ dysfunction from a dysregulated host response to infection* — not an isolated vital-sign abnormality. This is the clinical-definitions-level justification for why our device's fusion logic should score **breadth of systems affected simultaneously**, not just how far any one vital deviates.
- Both tools support the general principle used in the implementation plan's ML section (§7.1): **rolling-window, multi-parameter fusion scored over time is inherently more sepsis-specific than instantaneous threshold crossing**, because ordinary illness tends to perturb 1–2 systems that recover, while sepsis tends to progressively involve more systems that do not recover.

Sources: [Bedside PEWS multicentre validation, PMC3387627](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3387627/); [PEWS as sepsis identification tool, Frontier Med & Health Research](https://fmhr.net/index.php/fmhr/article/view/1317); [pSOFA comprehensive review, PMC11162817](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11162817/); [pSOFA validation in pediatric ED, PubMed 35575803](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35575803/); [Adaptation/validation of pSOFA and Sepsis-3 in critically ill children, PubMed 28783810](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28783810/)

## 4. Recommended Composite Suspicion Logic (for the implementation plan's ML/threshold section)

This directly extends implementation-plan §7.1 ("Threshold-and-trend fusion as a fallback/baseline"). Instead of independent WHO IMCI thresholds OR'd together, score a **weighted composite across differentiating features**, most of which are cheap to compute from data we already stream.

**Proposed feature set (all computable from existing PPG + temp + IMU sensors, no new hardware):**

1. **Temperature direction + rhythm score**
   - Flag both directions equally: `temp ≥ 38.0°C` OR `temp < 35.5°C` (existing IMCI thresholds — keep, since hypothermia is confirmed to be as sepsis-relevant as fever in neonates, not a secondary case).
   - Add a rolling **rhythm-regularity feature**: variance of temperature rate-of-change over a 4–6 hour window. A flattened/erratic curve (vs. a fever that rises-plateaus-falls in the expected diurnal shape) scores higher risk, even before a hard threshold is crossed. (Confidence: suggestive, adult-ICU-derived evidence; treat as a lower-weight bonus feature, not a primary trigger.)

2. **HR-temperature proportionality ratio**
   - Compute `Δ HR (bpm) / Δ Temp (°C)` relative to baseline for the current episode.
   - Expected band for simple fever: ~8–10 bpm rise per 1°C (Liebermeister's rule; wider tolerance band, e.g., 6–14 bpm/°C, to avoid over-triggering).
   - Score risk up if HR rise is **outside this band in either direction** — excess tachycardia (compensatory circulatory response) or relative bradycardia (Faget-sign-like pattern) — *and* especially if this deviation co-occurs with a temperature abnormality (don't score HR deviation alone; pair it with the temp signal).

3. **HRV composite pattern (highest-weight feature)**
   - Compute the HeRO-style triad from PPG-derived RR-intervals over a rolling 6–12 hour window: (a) reduced HRV (SDNN/RMSSD below rolling personal baseline), (b) deceleration frequency/asymmetry (transient HR drops), (c) reduced sample entropy (signal becoming pathologically more regular, not just lower-amplitude).
   - Score risk only when **multiple of the three sub-components trend together and persist/worsen over the window** — a momentary dip during sleep or feeding should not trigger; sustained multi-hour co-degradation should.
   - Explicitly do **not** treat "HRV is low right now" as sepsis-specific on its own — viral illness also lowers HRV. Require the *combination and trend*, per the evidence above.

4. **Perfusion index trend**
   - Compute PI (pulsatile/non-pulsatile PPG amplitude ratio) as a rolling baseline-relative trend, not absolute value (absolute PI norms are poorly established for neonates on a limb-worn low-cost sensor).
   - A **declining PI trend over hours**, especially co-occurring with HR/HRV abnormality, scores as one of the highest-weight features — mechanistically this is the closest proxy we have to "shock," which common illness does not produce.
   - Flag explicitly in the demo/pitch that PI-sepsis linkage is validated in adult septic shock literature, not neonatal wearables — this is our most promising but least-validated-for-this-age-group signal, and a clear candidate for the device's own future clinical validation study.

5. **Respiratory irregularity, not just rate**
   - From the same IMU periodicity signal already used for RR-proxy, add an **inter-breath-interval variance / apnea-pause detection** feature, not just breaths/min.
   - `RR ≥ 60/min AND regular rhythm` → weight as illness-consistent (URI-pattern).
   - `RR ≥ 60/min AND irregular/apneic pattern` OR `apnea/bradycardia event frequency increasing` → weight substantially higher (sepsis-consistent pattern per the preterm-infant apnea/bradycardia predictive literature).

6. **Activity/lethargy trend, not snapshot**
   - Score low-activity readings against **response to expected recovery** (e.g., does activity rebound after a feeding/holding event, consistent with a comfort-responsive illness) vs. **flat/declining regardless of caregiver intervention** (sepsis-consistent).

7. **Breadth-of-systems scoring (the actual specificity lever)**
   - Rather than summing independent thresholds, weight the composite score up disproportionately when **≥3 of the above 6 feature groups are simultaneously abnormal and trending in the same (worsening) direction over a multi-hour window** — mirroring the PEWS/pSOFA multi-organ logic. A single abnormal parameter (e.g., isolated fever with normal HR ratio, normal HRV, normal PI, regular breathing, responsive activity) should stay Green/Amber, not Red — this is precisely the rule that keeps a common cold from tripping a Red/high-suspicion alert.
   - This "breadth + trend" gate is the concrete mechanism that answers the reviewer's question: a common illness perturbs 1, occasionally 2, of these 6 feature groups and resolves; sepsis tends to perturb 3+ simultaneously and does not resolve without treatment.

**Confidence framing for the pitch/demo:** Be explicit that this composite logic is a *research-informed hypothesis*, assembled from validated adult/NICU/ICU literature and general pediatric early-warning-score principles — not itself clinically validated as a bundle on a low-cost limb-worn sensor in neonates. The individual mechanisms (HRV/HeRO, PI/shock, Liebermeister's rule, WHO IMCI hypothermia data, PEWS/pSOFA multi-parameter logic) are each independently well-evidenced; their specific combination and weighting on this device is the thing that requires our own future validation study. This honesty is itself a strength in a judged setting — it shows the team understands the difference between "clinically plausible, well-sourced triage logic" and "diagnostic-grade proof," which is the correct posture for a screening triage aid per the device's own stated positioning (implementation plan §10).

## 5. Explicit limitations of a wearable-only approach

- **No blood pressure, lactate, white blood cell count, CRP/procalcitonin, or blood culture** — the actual clinical gold-standard sepsis workup components are entirely absent from this sensor set. Nothing here should be marketed or coded as a "sepsis diagnosis."
- **HeRO's own RCT performance** (68% sensitivity / ~11% specificity at single-threshold crossing) shows that even a clinically validated, decade-refined HRV algorithm in a controlled NICU environment does not achieve high standalone specificity — it works because it shifts clinician *attention* and combined with clinical judgment, not because it is a stand-alone rule-in test. Our composite logic should be expected to perform similarly: a sensitivity-favoring triage aid that increases the *base rate of suspicion* enough to prompt a caregiver/ASHA worker to seek care sooner, not a definitive positive/negative test.
- **Perfusion index and HRV entropy evidence for neonates specifically on limb-worn low-cost PPG hardware is thin** — most PI/shock evidence is adult ICU with clinical-grade pulse oximeters; most rigorous entropy/deceleration HRV evidence (HeRO) is preterm-infant NICU with clinical-grade ECG leads, not PPG on a low-cost wrist/ankle module in ambient (non-ICU) conditions. Motion artifact, ambient light, and skin perfusion differences in a home/rural setting are real engineering risks to signal quality that the underlying research does not address.
- **Circadian temperature-rhythm evidence is adult/ICU-derived**, not neonatal — plausible extension, not confirmed.
- Given all of the above, **the honest device claim is: "elevated composite, multi-parameter, trending suspicion of sepsis warranting urgent clinical evaluation" — not "detected sepsis."** This mirrors how BEMPU, Cradle VSA, and the SepAl paper itself frame their own outputs, and is consistent with the "screening triage aid" framing already in the implementation plan.
